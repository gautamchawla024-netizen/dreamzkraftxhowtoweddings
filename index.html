Reading the three options

  • Plan A (₹4.19L a month) beats every target: 1,153 venue, 792 destination and 253 planning QLs.
  • Plan B (₹3.12L a month) just clears all three: 101%, 103% and 104%.
  • Plan C (₹1.49L a month) is Meta only and lands 86% of venue, 81% of destination and 60% of planning. Its cost per QL is lowest because it carries no Google spend, not because Meta works harder.

4. How we run it

How we run it week to week

Stop and scale rules
  1. Grade every lead in the CRM within 24 hours and send the grade back to Meta and Google; campaigns are judged on share of very good leads, not cost per lead.
  2. Pause and refresh any line whose cost per QL stays more than 25% above plan for 14 days.
  3. Scale by 20% a week where cost per QL stays under plan with QL% at or above plan; avoid budget jumps above 30% so Meta does not restart learning.
  4. Keep QL-optimised ad sets few: one or two per campaign, so each gets the ~50 weekly signals Meta needs to learn.
  5. Week-four checkpoint: match Google leads to the CRM; above ₹3k per real lead, move that budget to Meta venue reels.
What has to hold for the plan to hold. A QL% of 20% on Meta Venues is a planning assumption: the CRM audit graded most past Meta leads as not a fit. November is the month that proves it. If CRM-graded QL% is under 12% by week three, we re-plan before December rather than spend into it.

Appendix: the expanded plan

Version 2 breaks each plan into platform and campaign. Version 3 adds every metric, month by month. The notes first explain how the budget moves between Google and Meta as the plans scale down.

Why Google pauses as we scale down, and why it should stay alive

  • Why it pauses in Plan C. Google costs ₹2,100–2,200 per QL against ₹540–600 on Meta. At ₹1.5L a month every rupee has to work hardest, and Google’s Rajasthan and Mumbai lines would each get too few clicks to learn after the leakage is blocked.
  • How Meta scales up to compensate. In Plan C, Meta spend rises to Plan A levels on Venues (within ~10%), sits between Plans B and A on Destination, and stays lean on Planning, where a lower spend buys the cheapest QLs. That keeps Plan C at 86% of the venue target with no Google at all.
  • Why Google matters anyway. Google is a pull medium: it reaches families who are already searching for a venue or a planner, so the intent is there before the ad. The old Jaipur and Udaipur pages converted 3.7–4.1% of clicks. Meta creates demand; Google captures it, and those leads are typically further along in the decision.
  • Our recommendation. If budget allows, keep a small always-on Google floor (brand plus premium Rajasthan terms) even in the leanest plan, so that search demand created by the Meta reels has somewhere to land.
